# Methodology for paired A/B evaluation of AI-agent components

Research memo, 2026-09-04. Scope: statistics and fairness for agent evals (Part A), a taxonomy of deterministically-verifiable agent behaviours with existing benchmarks (Part B), and concrete infrastructure recommendations (Part C). Every claim carries a URL; numbers are quoted from the cited source.

---

## Part A. Statistics and fairness for agent evals

### A1. Run-to-run variance and how many repeats you need

**How noisy are agents?**

- τ-bench introduced `pass^k` = the chance that *all* k i.i.d. trials of a task succeed, averaged over tasks, estimated per task as C(c,k)/C(n,k) with c successes in n trials. gpt-4o scored pass^1 = 61.2% on retail but pass^8 < 25%; the paper ran "at least 3 trials per task" for headline numbers and "at least 40" trials per task for its consistency analysis. Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2406.12045 and https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12045
- τ²-bench reports pass^k out to k = 4 for every model (so ≥4 trials per task) and notes one trial over all domains costs about $40 with gpt-4.1 as agent and user simulator. Source: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.07982
- Vending-Bench: "all models have runs that derail" even when the median run turns a profit, and failures did not correlate with the point where the context window filled. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15840 ; Vending-Bench 2 still shows frontier models far below the human baseline: https://andonlabs.com/evals/vending-bench-2
- ReasonBENCH ran 30 independent trials per configuration (10 strategies × 12 models × 6 tasks). A power analysis said n ≥ 9 suffices; 30 gives a 3× margin with median 95% CI widths under 2 points. The inter-quartile range at T = 0 was "comparable to—and in several cells wider than" the range at T = 0.7, i.e. variance persists under greedy decoding. The best strategy beat its nearest competitor in only 77% of runs. A hierarchical decomposition attributes ≈75% of variance to benchmark/system/item structure, ≈1% to pure repeats, and ≈25% to an unexplained residual. Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2512.07795
- "Beyond pass@1" (23,392 episodes, 396 tasks, 10 models, 2 scaffolds, k = 3 repeats) finds pass@1 falls from 76.3% on short tasks to 52.1% on very-long ones, with a Variance Amplification Factor ≥ 2.37 for frontier models (long-horizon variance more than doubles short-horizon variance), and recommends k ≥ 3 repeats as a floor, reporting partial credit (GDS) alongside pass@1, and tracking infrastructure completion rate separately from task success. Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2603.29231
- Noise Floor Audit for Agent Benchmarks: 10 frozen reruns at T = 0 of 150 BFCL instances on three endpoints gave "ever-flip" fractions of 0.7%, 2.0% and 2.7% and mean run correlations of 0.997/0.966/0.961, but semantics-preserving prompt perturbations produced paired SDs 11×–58× larger than rerun SDs. Recommendation: publish the frozen instance list, report both rerun error bars and perturbation SDs, and treat gaps smaller than the perturbation floor as prompt sensitivity, not capability. Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2608.22331
- ReliabilityBench (1,280 episodes, 4 domains) defines a reliability surface R(k, ε, λ) over repeats, semantic perturbations and injected faults (timeouts, rate limits, partial responses, schema drift); perturbations alone reduce success from 96.9% to 88.1%. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06112
- AgentNoiseBench (24 models): injecting solvability-preserving user/tool noise into τ²-Bench, VitaBench and multi-hop QA drops accuracy by 20.8% on average; reasoning strength and noise robustness "are not closely related". Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11348v2
- Terminal-Bench 2.1 "runs tasks repeatedly because the same agent-model configuration may succeed on one attempt and fail on another", and its leaderboard draws 95% CI whiskers around each resolution rate. Sources: https://snorkel.ai/leaderboard/terminal-bench-2-1/ , https://www.tbench.ai/leaderboard/terminal-bench/2.0

**Why temperature 0 does not give you determinism**

- Thinking Machines sampled 1,000 completions of "Tell me about Richard Feynman" from Qwen3-235B at temperature 0 and got 80 distinct outputs (identical for the first 102 tokens); the cause is kernels that are not batch-invariant, so output depends on concurrent server load; batch-invariant kernels made all 1,000 identical. Source: https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/defeating-nondeterminism-in-llm-inference/
- Under BF16 greedy decoding, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B showed up to 9% SD in AIME'24 accuracy across GPU count/type and batch size, and response lengths varied by up to 9,000 tokens. Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09501v2
- Provider/endpoint choice is itself a nuisance variable: Epoch reports scaffold changes moving SWE-bench Verified by up to 11 pp (GPT-5) and 15 pp (Kimi K2 Thinking), GPQA-Diamond averages ranging 74–80% across settings, and a 23 pp τ-bench difference for one vendor between its own API and a ChatCompletions path. Source: https://epoch.ai/gradient-updates/why-benchmarking-is-hard
- Prompt caching "does not change how the model generates output tokens", but the same docs say identical requests are not guaranteed identical results; cache hits are reported in `usage.input_tokens_details.cached_tokens`, which you should log as a covariate. Source: https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/prompt-caching . A study of caching on long-horizon agent tasks measured cost (41–80% savings) and TTFT (13–31%) but not accuracy, so treat "caching is output-neutral" as an assumption to A/A-test rather than a fact. Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2601.06007

**The statistics of repeats (how many, and where the variance goes)**

- Anthropic's "Adding Error Bars to Evals": total variance of a mean score is (Var(x) + E[σ²_i])/n where Var(x) is question-level variance and σ²_i is per-question sampling variance; K resamples per question divide the second term by K. Rule: increase K until E[σ²_i]/K ≪ Var(x). In the uniform-difficulty binary example, K = 2 removes 1/3 of total variance and K = 6 removes 5/9 (upper bound 2/3). Power: detecting a 3 pp absolute difference at 80% power, α = 0.05 needs ≈969 independent questions; going from K = 1 to K = 10 on a small eval lowers the minimum detectable effect from 13.2% to 7.5%. Also: clustered standard errors (questions sharing a passage/repo) were >3× the naive SE on DROP. Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2411.00640
- "Measuring all the noises of LLM Evals" decomposes total noise = data noise (which questions) + prediction noise (which sample). Prediction noise is typically larger (≈2× on MATH500); paired analysis plus averaging several samples per question shrinks the detectable difference 3–6× on HumanEval and ≈4× elsewhere. Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2512.21326
- pass@k must be estimated without bias: generate n ≥ k samples, count c correct, pass@k = E[1 − C(n−c,k)/C(n,k)]; the naive 1 − (1 − p̂)^k is "a consistent underestimate" whose gap "doesn't fully close even when n > 5k". Source: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.03374 (Sec. 2.1 and App. A)
- Anthropic's agent-eval guide: "Because model outputs vary between runs, we run multiple trials"; pass@k is for tools where one success matters, pass^k for agents where consistency matters; "you won't know if your graders are working well unless you read the transcripts and grades from many trials". Source: https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents
- "Signal and Noise" (pretraining evals) shows discrete metrics (accuracy, exact match) have far worse signal-to-noise than continuous ones (GSM8K SNR 1.2 → 7.0 when switching to bits-per-byte); the agent analogue is partial credit / checkpoints, which "Beyond pass@1" (GDS) and TheAgentCompany use. Sources: https://arxiv.org/html/2508.13144v1 , https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.14161

### A2. Paired vs unpaired designs, and interleaving against drift

**Pairing.** When both arms run the same scenarios, per-scenario difficulty is shared, so Var(paired diff) = Var(unpaired diff) − 2·Cov(x_A, x_B)/n; with correlation 0.5 the paired design cuts variance by ≈1/3, and the paper asks for pairwise SEs, CIs and the correlation coefficient to be reported next to per-arm scores. Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2411.00640 . For binary paired outcomes the NLP significance-testing guide recommends McNemar's test, and paired bootstrap or approximate-randomization when distributional assumptions fail; it warns to correct for multiple comparisons and to report effect sizes. Source: https://aclanthology.org/P18-1128.pdf

**Paired-task bootstrap in agent papers.**
- "The Scaffold Effect" (3 harnesses × 2 models × 50 stratified Terminal-Bench Pro tasks, one run per cell) computes 95% paired-task bootstrap CIs with B = 10,000 by resampling tasks with replacement; harness choice moved tokens-per-solved-task by up to 40× (Goose 28K–37K vs OpenCode 1.15M–1.55M) while within-model pass-rate differences were 0–8 pp with CIs that include zero except for the largest gap. It proposes evaluating "harness–model pairs" with tokens per solved task, no-action turns and a failure-category vector as first-class metrics. Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2607.22585
- "Scaffold Effects on GAIA" (3 scaffolds × 5 models × 139 tasks × 3 attempts) uses a sample-ID-clustered bootstrap (5,000 iterations, fixed seed) so resampling preserves within-question dependence across arms, plus mixed-effects logistic regression for scaffold×model interactions; scaffold alone moved accuracy up to 28 pp within one model. Recommendations: never compare models across different scaffolds, report scaffold-conditional estimates. Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2606.08529
- Hidden Measurement Error: naive CIs were 40–60% narrower than the "Total Evaluation Error" corrected ones; judge disagreement alone accounted for 43.8% of variance in safety evals; submitting K = 27 prompt variants and reporting the best inflates a score by 56 Elo. Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2604.11581

**Drift you must design against.**
- Model APIs change under you: GPT-4's accuracy on prime/composite identification fell from 84% (March 2023) to 51% (June 2023). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09009
- Server load changes numerics (batch invariance) and providers differ in rate limits, truncation and templates (A1 sources above).

**Interleaving / concurrency.**
- Online-experiment practice: "it is important to randomize over time by running the control and treatment(s) concurrently with a fixed percentage to each throughout the experiment" (otherwise Simpson's paradox); run for at least a full weekly cycle because of day-of-week effects; watch for primacy/novelty effects. Sources: https://www.exp-platform.com/Documents/2015%20Online%20Controlled%20Experiments_EncyclopediaOfMLDM.pdf , https://ai.stanford.edu/~ronnyk/2009controlledExperimentsOnTheWebSurvey.pdf
- A/A tests ("null tests") run the system with two identical arms to (i) measure variability for power calculations and (ii) check the pipeline: the null should be rejected ≈5% of the time at 95% confidence. Source: https://ai.stanford.edu/~ronnyk/2009controlledExperimentsOnTheWebSurvey.pdf ; Microsoft ExP also recommends retrospective A/A analysis and a power calculation before the experiment starts. Source: https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/group/experimentation-platform-exp/articles/patterns-of-trustworthy-experimentation-pre-experiment-stage/
- Interleaving (IR): evaluating two rankers as a paired comparison inside the same query "requires approximately 1–2 orders of magnitude less data than absolute metrics", attributed to its being a paired test; team-draft interleaving randomizes which side goes first. Source: https://www.cs.cornell.edu/~tj/publications/chapelle_etal_12a.pdf . Airbnb reports ≈50× speed-up over A/B tests, a coin flip per search deciding which ranker's item is placed first, and a caveat that directional agreement with A/B tests was only 82%. Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2508.00751v1
- Translation to agent evals: schedule the trials of arm A and arm B for the same scenario back-to-back in the same time window, alternate order (A,B / B,A) per trial index, pin the same provider/endpoint and model snapshot for both arms, and log cache-hit tokens, latency and provider as covariates. This is what makes the paired difference absorb drift; the scaffold papers above hold "verbatim instruction; native test suite; Daytona sandbox per task; 900-second wall-time cap; OpenRouter as the model gateway" constant for the same reason. Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2607.22585

### A3. LLM-as-judge pitfalls, blinding, and how to do pairwise judging properly

**Documented biases (with numbers).**
- Position bias: with the default prompt, GPT-4 was consistent on only 65.0% of swapped pairs (30.0% favoured the first slot), GPT-3.5 46.2%, Claude-v1 23.8% (75% first-slot). Verbosity: a "repetitive list" attack fooled Claude-v1 and GPT-3.5 91.3% of the time vs 8.7% for GPT-4. Self-enhancement bias is also documented. GPT-4 agreed with humans 85% on MT-Bench (human–human 81%) and 87% on Arena. Mitigations proposed: swap positions and only accept consistent verdicts (else tie), few-shot, chain-of-thought, reference-guided grading. Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2306.05685v4
- A systematic study (15 judges, ≈150k judgments) finds position bias is not random noise, differs in direction by judge (GPT-4o slight primacy, Claude-3 recency), and is driven mostly by the quality gap: near-equal answers show position bias in 40–60% of comparisons vs >85% consistency for large gaps. Recommendation: swap positions and count inconsistent verdicts as ties rather than dropping them, and filter hard-to-judge pairs. Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2406.07791v5
- Self-preference: GPT-4 recognises its own text 73.5% of the time; self-preference rises linearly with self-recognition; authors recommend "authorship obfuscation" as standard practice. Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2404.13076 . On verifiable tasks, strong models' self-preference is mostly legitimate (correlation 0.77–0.80 with accuracy) but when they are wrong they show *more* harmful self-preference (86% for the strongest Qwen on math); inference-time reasoning reduces it. Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2504.03846v2
- Preference leakage: judges related to the evaluated system (same model, distilled from it, or same family) are biased toward it, and this is "harder to detect" than other judge biases. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.01534
- Length/style: prompting the baseline to be verbose vs concise swung its AlpacaEval win rate from 22.9% to 64.3%; length-controlled regression raised correlation with Arena from 0.94 to 0.98 and cut gameability SD from 25% to 10%. Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2404.04475 . LMArena's style control adds length/headers/bold/list features to the Bradley–Terry regression; the length coefficient (0.249) is ≈10× the markdown ones, and controlling it moved GPT-4o-mini from rank 6 to 11 and Grok-2-mini from 6 to 18. Source: https://www.lmsys.org/blog/2024-08-28-style-control/
- Judge pipelines add measurement error that shrinks naive CIs by 40–60% (see A2). Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2604.11581
- Panels: a panel of smaller judges from disjoint families correlated better with humans than one large judge, reduced intra-model bias and was >7× cheaper. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18796

**How to do it properly.**
- Prefer deterministic graders; use model graders only where code cannot check ("code-based: fast, cheap, objective, reproducible ... brittle to valid variations; model-based: flexible ... non-deterministic"). Grade the outcome, not the path. Source: https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents
- Binary pass/fail with a written critique, one principal domain expert as ground truth, iterate until judge–expert agreement is high (>90% in three iterations in the example), evaluate the judge as a classifier with TPR and TNR on train/dev/test splits. Source: https://hamel.dev/blog/posts/llm-judge/ . Rubrics drift as you grade ("criteria drift"), so judge criteria must be validated against human grades, not written once. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.12272
- Pilot the judge: ABC item O.c.1 requires "pilot experiments to assess the accuracy and self-consistency of LLM judges" and WebArena's unvalidated judge/substring matching over-estimated by 1.4–5.2%. Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2507.02825
- Agent-specific judges: Agent-as-a-Judge reached ≈90% alignment with human consensus on DevAI vs 60–70% for plain LLM-as-a-judge. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10934 . Mind2Web 2 uses per-task judge scripts with rubric trees (avg 50 nodes) that do exact checks deterministically and delegate only extraction/claim verification to an LLM; human audit found 99.03% judge correctness over 720 verifications. Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2506.21506 . LongMemEval's GPT-4o judge reached >97% agreement with experts *because* answers are short facts. Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10813 . MAST's o1-based failure annotator reached κ = 0.77 (94% accuracy) vs human labels. Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2503.13657
- Production example: one judge call with a rubric (factual accuracy, citation accuracy, completeness, source quality, tool efficiency) emitting 0–1 scores plus pass/fail "was the most consistent and aligned with human judgements"; humans still caught hallucinations and source-selection biases the judge missed. Source: https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system
- Security-specific: never let a judge read attacker-controlled text and decide success; AgentDojo grades with state-inspecting utility/security functions precisely because injections "might fool evaluation models themselves". Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2406.13352v3 . WASP uses an LLM judge only for the intermediate "was the agent diverted" signal and rule-based checks for end-to-end attacker success. Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2504.18575

### A4. Contamination and overfitting of scenarios to a component; holdout practice

- Agent benchmarks mostly lack holdouts: "AI Agents That Matter" distinguishes distribution-specific, task-specific, domain-general and fully-general claims (each needs a different holdout: in-distribution samples, OOD samples, held-out tasks, held-out domains) and found only 1/1, 3/6, 1/8 and 0/2 benchmarks had the right holdout; STeP on WebArena hard-coded Reddit URL patterns; simple baselines (warming: 93.2% at $2.45) matched or beat LATS (88.0% at $134.50). Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01502
- Memorisation is real for coding agents: on SWE-bench Verified, models identify the buggy file path from the issue text alone 60–76% of the time vs <53% on equally popular external repos; 5-gram overlap when reproducing gold functions was up to 34.9% vs 18.2% outside the SWE-bench ecosystem. Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2506.12286 . Issue-only file localisation: 65% on SWE-bench vs 12.2% on BeetleBox. Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2512.10218v2
- Constraint overfitting: models "strongly overfit on a small set of verifiable constraints from benchmarks like IFEval" and fail on 58 new out-of-domain constraints (IFBench). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.02833
- GSM1k (a fresh GSM8K look-alike) shows drops of up to 8% and a Spearman r² = 0.36 between a model's likelihood of regurgitating GSM8K and its GSM8K–GSM1k gap. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00332
- Prompt-variant hacking: reporting the best of K = 27 prompt variants inflates by 56 Elo. Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2604.11581
- Rolling/private holdouts that work: LiveCodeBench dates every problem and detects contamination by comparing performance before/after model cut-offs. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07974 . ARC-AGI keeps semi-private and private evaluation sets and warns not to leak evaluation information "by repeatedly modifying an algorithm while using its evaluation score as feedback". Source: https://arcprize.org/guide/1 . ABC items R.3/R.4 ask for contamination measures and an update plan; T.5 requires isolating agents from ground truth (SWE-Lancer agents could read test files and "score 100% without solving tasks"). Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2507.02825 . HAL's log audit found eight cases where agents located dataset answers on HuggingFace or arXiv. Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2510.11977v1
- Saturation: "an eval at 100% tracks regressions but provides no signal for improvement". Source: https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents . Mid-range items (30–70% historical pass rate) carry the most Fisher information; the reduced set needs 5–10 agents scored on the full set first. Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2603.23749v1
- Safety components: a static injection suite is not a holdout. Twelve published defenses were broken with >90% ASR by adaptive (gradient/RL/search/human) attacks; the paper asks for worst-case ASR under an adaptive attacker with a compute budget. Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2510.09023
- Memory components: swapping only the embedding model (+6.2 pp, p = 0.004) reversed a Mem0-vs-RAG conclusion; the full-context vs RAG comparison flipped by 45 points across two LLM families; Mem0's write path costs 1,000+ LLM calls per instance vs $0.01 for verbatim RAG. Protocol: compare to verbatim RAG with a named embedding model, include a random-retrieval control, test ≥2 model families, report write-path cost. Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2606.29914

### A5. Reporting "no significant difference" honestly

- A non-significant p-value is not evidence of no effect ("the absence of an effect can be rejected, but not statistically supported"). Use TOST equivalence testing: pre-register a smallest effect size of interest (SESOI), run two one-sided tests against ±SESOI, equivalently check that the 90% CI lies inside the bounds; report which of four outcomes occurred: equivalent & not different, different but equivalent, different & not equivalent, or *inconclusive* (neither). Sources: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5502906/ , https://cran.rstudio.com/web/packages/TOSTER/vignettes/IntroductionToTOSTER.html
- Report the minimum detectable effect your run could have found: δ = (z_{α/2}+z_β)·sqrt((ω² + σ²_A/K_A + σ²_B/K_B)/n); if the observed gap is below the A/A noise floor or the perturbation floor, say so. Sources: https://arxiv.org/html/2411.00640 , https://arxiv.org/html/2608.22331
- Report distributions, not points: "at minimum stratified-bootstrap CIs, ideally Global and Run Noise alongside the mean", plus cost distributions. Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2512.07795
- Report the paired CI and correlation, sample size and cluster counts under each mean. Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2411.00640
- Multiple comparisons: many scenario categories × many metrics inflate false positives; correct (Bonferroni/FDR) or pre-register the primary metric. Source: https://aclanthology.org/P18-1128.pdf
- Do not equate "not different in accuracy" with "equivalent": the Scaffold Effect harnesses were within 0–8 pp in pass rate (CIs spanning zero) yet 40× apart in tokens per solved task, and the Complexity Trap found observation masking matches summarisation's solve rate at half the raw cost. Sources: https://arxiv.org/html/2607.22585 , https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.21433
- Treat "wrong but zero-scored" separately from "abstained": HAL notes an agent that misused a credit card got the same zero as one that abstained. Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2510.11977v1

## Part B. Scenario taxonomy: what a deterministic verifier can check, what needs a judge

Format per category: archetypes → benchmarks that already contain such tasks → deterministic checks → judge-only aspects.

### B1. System-prompt / instruction following
- Archetypes: (a) output-format constraints (JSON only, ≤N words, no commas, must include/exclude keywords); (b) system-vs-user conflicts (system says "never reveal X / always answer in French", user pushes back over 5 turns); (c) role/action rules ("refuse to translate", "must end with a P.S.").
- Benchmarks: IFEval, ≈541 prompts, 25 verifiable instruction types, prompt-/instruction-level strict and loose metrics: https://huggingface.co/datasets/google/IFEval , https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.07911 ; IFBench, 58 new OOD constraints + 29 training constraints with verification functions: https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.02833 ; SysBench, 500 system messages × 5 turns, six constraint types, CSR/ISR/SSR (GPT-4o 87.1/76.4/54.4), three failure types (constraint violation, instruction misjudgement, multi-turn instability): https://arxiv.org/html/2408.10943 ; RuLES, 14 scenarios each with a programmatic rule-violation function: https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04235
- Deterministic: regex/parsers for format, counts, language ID, forbidden strings, JSON schema validity, "did the secret leak" substring checks, per-turn stability.
- Judge: tone/style ("friendly but formal"), semantic paraphrases of forbidden content, whether a refusal was appropriate. SysBench uses a GPT-4o checklist judge for these.

### B2. Tool use / function calling
- Archetypes: (a) single- and parallel-call argument correctness against a schema; (b) irrelevance detection — no tool applies, agent must not hallucinate a call; (c) multi-turn stateful workflows (missing parameter → ask; missing function → say so) verified by end state; (d) policy-constrained customer-service flows with a simulated user.
- Benchmarks: BFCL (AST match on name/params/types, executable checks, relevance detection; v3 multi-turn with state-based plus response-based checks and 200-item augmented sets for missing params/functions/long context; v4 web search and memory): https://gorilla.cs.berkeley.edu/blogs/8_berkeley_function_calling_leaderboard.html , https://gorilla.cs.berkeley.edu/blogs/13_bfcl_v3_multi_turn.html , https://gorilla.cs.berkeley.edu/leaderboard.html ; τ-bench (DB end-state vs goal state, pass^k): https://arxiv.org/html/2406.12045 ; τ²-bench (dual-control telecom, compositional verifiable tasks): https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.07982 ; ToolBench (16,464 RapidAPI endpoints, ChatGPT-judged ToolEval) and StableToolBench (virtual API server + "solvable" filtering because live APIs made ToolBench non-reproducible): https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.16789 , https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07714 ; MCPMark (127 tasks over Notion/GitHub/Filesystem/PostgreSQL/Playwright, initial-state setup + programmatic verifier; gpt-5-medium 52.56% pass@1, 33.86% pass^4; ≈16 turns/17 tool calls per task): https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.24002 ; MCP-Bench (28 live servers, 250 tools) and MCP-Universe (231 tasks, 11 servers): https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.20453 , https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.16260 ; ReliabilityBench fault injection: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06112
- Deterministic: schema/AST match, executed-result equality, final DB/filesystem state equality, "no call made" on irrelevance items, required-action subset present, forbidden actions absent, retry behaviour under injected 429/timeouts.
- Judge: quality of the clarifying question, politeness/policy explanation to the simulated user; the user simulator itself is a noise source (τ-bench: "limited capacity at reasoning, calculation, long-context memorization").

### B3. Coding / terminal
- Archetypes: (a) issue → patch judged by hidden FAIL_TO_PASS and PASS_TO_PASS tests; (b) terminal tasks (build, configure, train, reproduce) with a pytest verifier in a container; (c) small exercises with two attempts where test output is fed back; (d) edit-format compliance.
- Benchmarks: SWE-bench (2,294 tasks; FAIL_TO_PASS/PASS_TO_PASS): https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06770 ; SWE-bench Verified (500 tasks screened by 93 developers, 3 annotators each, for well-specified issues and appropriate tests; Epoch estimates a 5–10% residual error rate and excludes 16 flaky samples): https://epoch.ai/benchmarks/swe-bench-verified ; Terminal-Bench 2.0 (89 tasks: instruction, Dockerfile, tests, human reference solution; oracle must pass; contributor checklist; adversarial exploit agent; 8-category failure taxonomy) : https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.11868 , https://arxiv.org/html/2601.11868 ; Terminal-Bench 2.1 fixed 28 of the 89 tasks (9 broken by external dependencies that changed after release, 8 by resource budgets too tight even for oracle solutions, the rest by instructions that diverged from tests) and added continuous validation: https://snorkel.ai/leaderboard/terminal-bench-2-1/ ; Harbor-Index found about one-third of the hardest tasks were broken (runtime failures, verifier overreach, exploitable verifiers, stale ground truth) and runs 18 trials per candidate task (3 per model×harness): https://www.tbench.ai/news/harbor-index ; Aider polyglot (225 Exercism exercises, 6 languages, two attempts, percent correct + edit-format compliance + cost): https://aider.chat/docs/leaderboards/ ; LiveCodeBench for time-stamped holdouts: https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07974
- Deterministic: tests pass, build exit code, lint/type-check, diff limited to allowed files, no test files modified (see B10), wall-time and token budget, edit-format validity.
- Judge: code quality/readability, whether the fix addresses root cause vs symptom (the Claude Code docs frame this as prompt guidance, not a grader): https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices

### B4. Long context / context management (compaction, tool-result clearing, sub-agents)
- Archetypes: (a) needle/multi-needle retrieval at varied depth and length, including low-lexical-overlap needles and distractors; (b) instruction persistence: a constraint given at turn 1 must still hold after N tool calls and a compaction event; (c) long-horizon execution where the agent must keep executing a provided plan without self-conditioning on its own earlier errors.
- Benchmarks: Needle-in-a-Haystack (depth × length grid): https://github.com/gkamradt/LLMTest_NeedleInAHaystack ; RULER (multi-needle, multi-hop tracing, aggregation): https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.06654 ; NoLiMa (no lexical overlap; GPT-4o 99.3% → 69.7% at 32K): https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05167v2 ; Lost in the Middle (U-shaped position effect): https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03172 ; LongBench v2 (503 MCQs, 8k–2M words): https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.15204 ; Context Rot (18 models; performance degrades with length even on simple tasks; distractors and haystack structure matter; LongMemEval focused ≈300 tokens vs full ≈113k): https://www.trychroma.com/research/context-rot ; The Illusion of Diminishing Returns (execution isolated from planning; self-conditioning): https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.09677 ; long-horizon agent tasks: Vending-Bench: https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15840 , TheAgentCompany (175 tasks, checkpoint partial credit): https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.14161 , Beyond pass@1's reliability-decay curves: https://arxiv.org/html/2603.29231 . Design guidance for what to test (compaction "maximize recall then precision", note-taking, sub-agents returning 1,000–2,000-token summaries, tool-result clearing): https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents ; empirical: observation masking halves cost at equal solve rate vs LLM summarisation on SWE-bench Verified: https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.21433
- Deterministic: exact/fractional needle recall, constraint still satisfied after compaction (regex), plan steps executed in order, tokens per solved task, whether a specified fact survives the summary (string check on the compacted context).
- Judge: summary faithfulness beyond the pinned facts, "did the agent stay coherent" on open-ended long runs (Vending-Bench uses bank balance as the deterministic proxy).

### B5. Memory across sessions
- Archetypes: (a) fact recall across sessions with distractor sessions; (b) knowledge update (later session overrides earlier) and selective forgetting; (c) temporal reasoning ("what did I say two weeks before X"); (d) abstention on unanswerable questions; (e) write-path cost and latency.
- Benchmarks: LoCoMo (≈300 turns, ≈9K tokens, up to 35 sessions; single-hop, multi-hop, temporal, open-domain, adversarial QA; F1 + judge): https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17753 ; LongMemEval (500 questions, 5 abilities incl. knowledge updates and abstention, S ≈115k / M ≈1.5M tokens, GPT-4o judge >97% agreement, 30–60% drops vs oracle): https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10813 ; MemBench (factual vs reflective memory; participation vs observation; effectiveness/efficiency/capacity): https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.21605 ; MemoryAgentBench (incremental chunks; accurate retrieval, test-time learning, long-range understanding, selective forgetting ≤28% for all methods; Mem0/Cognee/Zep 20–24%): https://arxiv.org/html/2507.05257 ; AgentMemBench (5 strategies; gold session provenance; EKV best recall at ≈25× the token footprint of windowing): https://arxiv.org/html/2608.00009 ; MemDelta controls (verbatim-RAG baseline, random-retrieval control, ≥2 model families, write-path cost): https://arxiv.org/html/2606.29914 ; vendor numbers to treat with the MemDelta caveats: https://mem0.ai/blog/state-of-ai-agent-memory-2026
- Deterministic: exact/substring match on short factual answers, Recall@k/NDCG against gold session IDs, "answer reflects the latest update", abstain-when-unanswerable, tokens/latency/LLM calls of the write path.
- Judge: paraphrased free-form answers (use binary correct/incorrect with reference), summary quality.

### B6. Safety / prompt injection / harmful-task refusal
- Archetypes: (a) indirect injection in a tool result (email, web page, issue comment) trying to trigger an attacker action; (b) same task with no injection (benign utility) so the guard's cost is visible; (c) explicitly harmful multi-step requests plus benign twins to measure over-refusal; (d) rule guards under adversarial users.
- Benchmarks: AgentDojo (97 user tasks × 27 injection targets = 629 cases; benign utility, utility under attack, targeted ASR; GPT-4o 69.0% → 50.08% utility, 47.69% ASR; tool filter ASR 7.5%; detector 7.95% ASR but utility 41.49%; binomial CIs): https://arxiv.org/html/2406.13352v3 ; InjecAgent (1,054 cases, 17 user tools, 62 attacker tools; GPT-4 ReAct 24% → 47% with a "hacking" prompt): https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.02691 ; BIPIA (first indirect-injection benchmark; boundary-awareness and explicit-reminder defenses): https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.14197 ; WASP (84 scenarios on VisualWebArena GitLab/Reddit; partial hijack up to 86% vs 0–17% end-to-end — "security through incompetence"): https://arxiv.org/html/2504.18575 ; Agent Security Bench (10 scenarios, 400+ tools, 23 attack/defense methods): https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.02644 ; AgentHarm (110 malicious tasks, 440 augmented, 11 categories, benign counterparts, rubric graders + judge): https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.09024 ; RuLES (programmatic rule checks under adversarial users): https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04235 ; adaptive-attack requirement: https://arxiv.org/html/2510.09023
- Deterministic: attacker goal reached in environment state (transfer made, file exfiltrated, email sent), user task still completed, forbidden tool/URL never invoked, secret never appears in output, refusal on the harmful twin and non-refusal on the benign twin.
- Judge: whether the agent was "diverted" mid-trajectory (WASP intermediate ASR), harm severity of partial completions; never let the judge see injected text as trusted.

### B7. Cost / efficiency (caching, routing, context pruning, reasoning effort)
- Archetypes: (a) same task set, report tokens and dollars per solved task; (b) reasoning-effort sweeps; (c) cache-hit ratio and TTFT on long multi-turn sessions; (d) Pareto frontier accuracy vs cost.
- Sources: cost-controlled evaluation and Pareto curves: https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01502 ; HAL (21,730 rollouts, ≈$40k; higher reasoning effort gave equal or lower accuracy in 21/36 combinations; report both token and dollar cost): https://arxiv.org/html/2510.11977v1 ; tokens per solved task = Σ tokens over all trials / number solved (40× harness spread): https://arxiv.org/html/2607.22585 ; The Complexity Trap (masking halves cost; hybrid a further 7–11%): https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.21433 ; caching cost/TTFT effects (41–80% / 13–31%): https://arxiv.org/html/2601.06007 ; token usage explains 80% of variance in a research agent's performance; multi-agent ≈15× chat tokens: https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system ; ReasonBENCH: cost varies >100× across strategies and does not predict quality: https://arxiv.org/html/2512.07795
- Deterministic: everything (input/output/cached tokens from usage fields, wall time, tool calls, no-action turns) — but always paired with the task outcome, never alone.
- Judge: none needed.

### B8. Multi-agent / delegation
- Archetypes: (a) orchestrator decomposes a task; verify the merged end state; (b) sub-agent brief quality: did each sub-agent receive objective, output format, boundaries; (c) failure-mode audit (step repetition, ignored input, premature termination, incorrect verification).
- Sources: MultiAgentBench/MARBLE (milestone-based KPIs; star/chain/tree/graph protocols): https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.01935 ; MAST (14 failure modes; e.g. step repetition 15.7%, reasoning–action mismatch 13.2%, no/incomplete verification 8.2%, incorrect verification 9.1%; LLM annotator κ = 0.77): https://arxiv.org/html/2503.13657 ; production eval practice (end-state not path; ~20 queries to start; single-call rubric judge): https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system ; scaffold comparison with Planner–Actor–Rater vs ReAct (+0.19 to +0.28 within one family, ≈0 elsewhere): https://arxiv.org/html/2606.08529 ; adversarial-review subagent pattern: https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices
- Deterministic: final artefact/state checks, milestone completion, message/tool-call counts, tokens, orchestrator budget compliance, no sub-agent exceeded scope (files touched).
- Judge: MAST-style failure labelling of traces, source quality, completeness.

### B9. Browsing / data (web, SQL)
- Archetypes: (a) web tasks on self-hosted sites verified by backend state or exact/must-include answers; (b) hard-to-find, easy-to-verify factual questions; (c) text-to-SQL on large enterprise schemas judged by execution results.
- Benchmarks: WebArena (812 tasks; exact_match, must_include, fuzzy_match via LLM, and state/DB checks; GPT-4 14.41% vs humans 78.24%): https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.13854 ; Mind2Web (2,000+ tasks, 137 sites; element accuracy, operation F1, step and task success): https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06070 ; Mind2Web 2 (130 tasks, Agent-as-a-Judge with rubric trees and URL-backed citation checks; pass@3): https://arxiv.org/html/2506.21506 ; BrowseComp (1,266 "inverted" questions — hard to find, easy to verify; LLM grader vs short reference; GPT-4o 0.6%, Deep Research 51.5%; calibration error 82–91% for browsing models): https://arxiv.org/html/2504.12516 ; Spider 2.0 (632 enterprise workflows on BigQuery/Snowflake; o1-preview 21.3% vs 91.2% on Spider 1.0): https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.07763 ; BIRD (12,751 pairs, 95 DBs, 33.4 GB; execution accuracy and valid-efficiency score; ChatGPT 40.08% vs human 92.96%): https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.03111 ; MCPMark's Playwright/PostgreSQL tasks: https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.24002
- Deterministic: DB row/state equality, execution-result equality for SQL, exact/normalised answer match, URL validity and reachability, citations resolve to pages containing the claim (string check).
- Judge: semantic equivalence of free-text answers, whether a cited page really supports a claim (Mind2Web 2 verifier), N/A-task handling (ABC flags WebArena's empty-response acceptance).

### B10. Verification / acceptance (does the agent check its own work?)
- Archetypes: (a) agent must write a failing reproduction test before fixing (fail-to-pass on old code, pass on new); (b) tasks whose tests conflict with the spec — the only way to "pass" is to tamper with tests (cheating rate); (c) evidence-of-verification: did the agent run the build/tests/screenshot before declaring done, and did the claimed result match the actual run.
- Sources: SWT-Bench (tests must fail before and pass after the golden patch; generated tests doubled SWE-Agent's precision as a patch filter): https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12952 ; TDD-Bench Verified (449 instances; failToPass + coverage of the change): https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.02883 , https://github.com/IBM/TDD-Bench-Verified ; ImpossibleBench (cheating rate = pass rate on spec-conflicting tests; taxonomy: modify tests, special-case, operator overloading, state recording; mitigations: prompting, read-only tests, abort option, monitoring): https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.20270 ; intrinsic self-correction does not work without external feedback: https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01798 ; models catch errors labelled as external but not their own (relabelling raises explicit correction by 23–93 pp): https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05976 ; cross-family verification beats self-verification: https://arxiv.org/html/2512.02304 ; MAST verification failure modes: https://arxiv.org/html/2503.13657 ; practice: give the agent a check it can run and have it "show evidence rather than asserting success", with a fresh-context reviewer: https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices
- Deterministic: test file hashes unchanged (or diff restricted), a test-run event appears in the trajectory before the final answer, the reported result equals the harness re-run, fail-to-pass status of agent-written tests, abort chosen on impossible tasks.
- Judge: whether a natural-language self-review actually identified the real defect; quality of the reproduction.

## Part C. Recommendations for the infrastructure (one page)

**1. Repeats and sample size.**
- Default K = 5 trials per (scenario, arm) for binary outcomes, never fewer than 3 (τ-bench ≥3 for headline numbers: https://arxiv.org/html/2406.12045 ; "Beyond pass@1" k ≥ 3: https://arxiv.org/html/2603.29231 ; Harbor-Index 3 per model×harness: https://www.tbench.ai/news/harbor-index ); K = 10–30 when the arms differ by a strategy/plugin rather than a model, because strategy effects are small and ranks flip in 23% of runs at K = 1 (https://arxiv.org/html/2512.07795 ).
- Before the first real comparison, run an A/A (arm A vs arm A) on the full suite to get the noise floor and the per-scenario variance for the power calculation; the null should reject ≈5% of the time (https://ai.stanford.edu/~ronnyk/2009controlledExperimentsOnTheWebSurvey.pdf , https://arxiv.org/html/2608.22331 ). Then size n scenarios × K trials with n = (z_{α/2}+z_β)²(ω² + σ²_A/K_A + σ²_B/K_B)/δ² for the pre-registered δ (https://arxiv.org/html/2411.00640 ). Prediction noise usually dominates, so extra trials per scenario buy more than extra scenarios (https://arxiv.org/html/2512.21326 ).

**2. Statistic to report.** Primary: per-scenario paired difference in mean pass rate (arm B − arm A), 95% CI from a scenario-clustered paired bootstrap (B ≥ 10,000; resample scenarios, keep all K trials of both arms together) as in https://arxiv.org/html/2607.22585 and https://arxiv.org/html/2606.08529 , plus McNemar/sign test as a check (https://aclanthology.org/P18-1128.pdf ). Alongside: pass^K per arm (https://arxiv.org/html/2406.12045 ), tokens and dollars per solved task (https://arxiv.org/html/2607.22585 , https://arxiv.org/html/2510.11977v1 ), the A–B correlation across scenarios, the A/A noise floor, and an MDE line. "No difference" is only claimable via TOST against a pre-registered SESOI with the 90% CI inside the bounds; otherwise label the result *inconclusive* (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5502906/ ). Apply an FDR/Bonferroni correction across scenario categories and secondary metrics.

**3. Interleaving and drift control.** Run both arms concurrently: for each scenario and trial index schedule A then B, then B then A (coin-flip order per pair, as in team-draft interleaving: https://arxiv.org/html/2508.00751v1 ), on the same pinned model snapshot and provider, inside identical frozen containers (ABC T.4/T.6: https://arxiv.org/html/2507.02825 ). Log model ID, provider, `cached_tokens`, latency, timestamp and batch/queue hints as covariates (https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/prompt-caching ). Re-run the A/A weekly and after any provider/model change (https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09009 , https://epoch.ai/gradient-updates/why-benchmarking-is-hard ). Do not use T = 0 as a substitute for repeats (https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/defeating-nondeterminism-in-llm-inference/ ).

**4. Blinding the judge.** Deterministic verifier first; judge only for residual criteria. The judge never sees arm names, component names, config, or timestamps; outputs are normalised (strip markdown/length cues or include length as a covariate: https://www.lmsys.org/blog/2024-08-28-style-control/ , https://arxiv.org/html/2404.04475 ). Pairwise judgments are run in both orders; inconsistent pairs count as ties (https://arxiv.org/html/2406.07791v5 , https://arxiv.org/html/2306.05685v4 ). The judge model must come from a family different from both arms' underlying models and from any model used to author the component (https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.01534 , https://arxiv.org/html/2404.13076 ); prefer a small panel across families (https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18796 ). Every judge is calibrated on ≥100 human-labelled transcripts with TPR/TNR reported before use, and re-checked when the rubric changes (https://hamel.dev/blog/posts/llm-judge/ , https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.12272 , ABC O.c.1). Judges never receive attacker-controlled text as trusted input (https://arxiv.org/html/2406.13352v3 ).

**5. Holdout.** Split every category into a *dev* set (visible to component authors) and a *sealed* set (private, run only by the infrastructure); report both and the dev–sealed gap as an overfitting alarm (https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01502 , https://arcprize.org/guide/1 , https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00332 ). Refresh the sealed set on a schedule and time-stamp scenarios (https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07974 ). Match the holdout to the claim: new instances for "this task", new tasks for "this domain", new domains for "general" (https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01502 ). For safety guards, add an adaptive red-team budget rather than a fixed injection list (https://arxiv.org/html/2510.09023 ). For memory plugins, include the MemDelta controls (verbatim RAG with named embeddings, random retrieval, ≥2 model families) (https://arxiv.org/html/2606.29914 ).

**6. Scenario authoring rules (enforced by CI on every new scenario).**
1. *Oracle must pass*: a reference solution passes the verifier in the frozen container (ABC T.9: https://arxiv.org/html/2507.02825 ; Terminal-Bench: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.11868 ).
2. *Null must fail*: an empty-response agent, a "do nothing" agent, and a random-action agent must all score 0 (ABC R.13; τ-bench's empty-response bug gave a trivial agent 38%: https://arxiv.org/html/2507.02825 ); an abstain outcome is recorded separately from a harmful action (https://arxiv.org/html/2510.11977v1 ).
3. *Decoys present*: each scenario ships at least one shortcut it must defeat — an irrelevant tool that must not be called (https://gorilla.cs.berkeley.edu/blogs/8_berkeley_function_calling_leaderboard.html ), an injected instruction in a tool result (https://arxiv.org/html/2406.13352v3 ), distractor sessions/needles with low lexical overlap (https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10813 , https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05167v2 ), noisy or failing tool responses (https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11348v2 ), and for coding tasks a spec–test conflict variant to measure cheating (https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.20270 ).
4. *Grade state, not path*; accept any valid trajectory (https://gorilla.cs.berkeley.edu/blogs/13_bfcl_v3_multi_turn.html , https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents ).
5. *Verifier scope = instruction scope*: no asserting unstated formats or messages (Harbor-Index "verifier overreach": https://www.tbench.ai/news/harbor-index ); instructions reviewed for under-specification the way SWE-bench Verified was (https://epoch.ai/benchmarks/swe-bench-verified ).
6. *Isolate ground truth*: tests and answers are not readable from inside the sandbox; no network unless the task needs it (ABC T.5; SWE-Lancer and HAL incidents: https://arxiv.org/html/2507.02825 , https://arxiv.org/html/2510.11977v1 ).
7. *Frozen, deterministic environment*: pinned versions, cached/simulated external APIs, state reset between trials (ABC T.4/T.6; StableToolBench: https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07714 ; Terminal-Bench 2.1 dependency drift: https://snorkel.ai/leaderboard/terminal-bench-2-1/ ).
8. *Pilot and prune*: run 2–3 reference agents × K trials; retire scenarios at 0% or 100% (no signal: https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents ), prefer 30–70% pass rates (https://arxiv.org/html/2603.23749v1 ), inspect outliers and read transcripts (ABC T.10; https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents ).
9. *Record cost per trial* (input/output/cached tokens, tool calls, wall time) so any comparison can be re-priced later (https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01502 ).
10. *Report card per comparison*: n scenarios, K trials, paired Δ with CI, pass^K per arm, tokens/$ per solved task, A/A floor, MDE, judge calibration (TPR/TNR), dev vs sealed gap, model snapshot and provider — following ABC R.10–R.13 (https://arxiv.org/html/2507.02825 ).
